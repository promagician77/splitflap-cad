Four perimeters of PLA at a 0.4mm nozzle is 1.6mm and still holds an M3 boss. That is the horizontal answer and it takes an afternoon.

The vertical gap does not respond to thinner walls at all. A flap pinned near its top edge sweeps a circle. Two cartridges stacked closer than that circle touch each other. Tab 3 lets you move the numbers and watch the circle move.

The two equations

hub_r = flap_count × slot_pitch / 2π

The hub has to be long enough around to hold the flaps side by side. Fewer flaps is the only thing that shrinks it.

sweep_d = 2 × (hub_r + flap_h − pin_inset)

The circle the flap tip traces. In a single depth plane, vertical pitch cannot go below this.

One thing the arithmetic says that your post does not

Your 30mm vertical gap implies a cartridge pitch near 76mm. The sweep circle on the assumptions above is 99.2mm. Those two cannot both be true, so one of my assumed inputs is wrong: the flap count, the pin inset, the hub, or the cartridges already overlap in depth. Finding out which is the first three hours of the job, and it is worth more than any guess I could make from here.

The parametric cartridge

Move anything. Every number and the drawing recompute. This is what the deliverable does, and it is why issue three stops being a third redesign.

Inputs

Side elevation, two stacked rows

Checks

Torque model is one flap held horizontal: m × g × (flap_h − pin_inset) / 2. The real drum is partly balanced so the true demand is lower, and friction plus acceleration push it back up. It reports 1.5% for the size that already works, which is the reason to believe it at 455%. Motor figure is the published 28BYJ-48 holding torque of 30 mN·m.

Bezel in front, cartridges offset behind

The one change that gets both gaps to the same small number, and the part that then becomes your wall mount.

How it works

  • A single flat bezel plate carries all the windows and is the only part anyone looks at.
  • Odd rows bolt to the front face of the rail, even rows to the back face. One cartridge, two mounting positions, same part.
  • Rows sharing a depth plane end up two pitches apart, which clears the sweep circle.
  • The gap you see becomes a bezel dimension. It is whatever you want it to be, both axes, and it is the same number in both.

What it costs

  • The back row sits roughly 20mm deeper, so its window narrows as you move off axis. Print coupon 4 before deciding.
  • A short light tunnel behind each back-row window, which wants a matte internal finish or it catches a highlight.
  • One more printed part in the stack, and a deeper piece overall.

This is your call, not mine. You are the one who knows whether a 20mm depth difference reads as a flaw or as a shadow box.

What a bigger grid actually needs

1
Cleat screws to the wall. Keyhole slots every 40mm, so it finds a stud wherever one is.
2
Rail hangs on the cleat, carries M3 holes at the cartridge pitch, front face and back face.
3
Cartridges bolt to the rail. Prints in 250mm sections and splices, so the grid is not limited by your bed.
4
Bezel clips on last. Tiles break on the web between windows, where a seam is invisible.

The load question nobody asks first

Weight scales with the grid and the fixings do not. A 4 × 4 of the current cartridge with motors is roughly 3kg hanging off two cleats.

Fine on a stud. Not fine on drywall anchors alone. It goes in the build note with a number next to it, because that is the kind of thing that only becomes a problem once the piece is hung.

Your three flap sizes are not three variants

Two of them are. The third is a different machine, and it is worth knowing that before issue one starts rather than after.

MetricToday 54×42.8Size B 108×42.8Size C 300×300

If you want size C anyway, and you might

1
Drop the flap count. An artwork probably wants 8 images, not 37. Stack mass falls from 3.5kg to about 755g and the hub shrinks with it.
2
Change the motor class. 136.6 mN·m is about a third of a typical NEMA 17, so it stops being marginal and starts being ordinary.
3
Change the flap stock. A 300mm span of 0.76mm PVC will not hold flat. Fluted polypropylene or foam core buys stiffness without the mass of thicker PVC.
4
Real bearings, steel shaft. A printed journal will not carry that much swinging mass for long.

None of that says impossible. It says size C should be quoted as its own build, after a short feasibility pass, instead of being priced as a resized cartridge and discovered later.

What gets printed before anything is committed

A printed mechanism fails on fits, not on dimensions. Four coupons, about ninety minutes of printing, and the model stops being a guess about your printer.

Two things worth saying plainly

The audit can come back and say 8mm vertical is not reachable without the depth offset. If that happens you hear it in hour three, not in week two, and you decide whether to spend the rest.
